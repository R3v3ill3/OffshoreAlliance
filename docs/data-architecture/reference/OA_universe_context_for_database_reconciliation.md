# Context: the OA Universe sector map and what it implies for the Offshore Alliance database

*Background wording for an agent reconciling the mapping files with the Offshore Alliance database. This section explains what the files contain, how they were built, and the design considerations the data raises. It contains no task instructions.*

---

## 1. What the mapping is

The **OA Universe** is a sector map of the Offshore Alliance's organising universe — the operators, assets, contractors and vessels in Australian offshore oil and gas — built to support organising strategy. It began as a three‑tab spreadsheet (onshore assets, offshore key assets, offshore contractors) and was extended in September 2026 through desk research, cross‑checking against a colleague's organiser‑whiteboard matrix, and manual verification by the map's owner. It now lives in one workbook, `OA universe.xlsx`, plus two adjacent files: the colleague's `WA_Offshore_Oil_Gas_Facilities_Contractor_Matrix.docx` (17 WA production facilities × production company × catering company × maintenance company) and a specification for a vessel‑tracking module.

The map was designed around **three vectors** that any row can be sliced on:

1. **Sub‑sector** — oil / gas & condensate / LNG / decommissioning, and (for services) the *scope of work*: catering, maintenance, HUC, inspection, cranes, helicopters, marine supply, crewing, ROV/subsea, drilling, construction/pipelay, decommissioning, survey.
2. **Tier‑1 (operator) footprint** — which titleholder/operator an asset or company sits under. The owner classifies operators as *Tier 1* (Chevron, Shell, Woodside, Inpex) and *Tier 2* (Santos, Jadestone, MODEC, BW Opal/BW Offshore).
3. **Contractor (employer) footprint** — which companies work across which assets and operators. The design driver is that a single contractor (Monadelphous is the canonical example) spans onshore and offshore facilities and several Tier‑1 operators.

A fourth dimension emerged during the work and is now explicit: **scope‑at‑facility** — *who holds which scope at which facility* (e.g. the catering incumbent on Goodwyn A), which is the level organisers actually work at.

## 2. The workbook, tab by tab

All statuses and presence are "as at September 2026". Every derived link carries a **confidence** (H / M / L) and a **source tag**: untagged = desk research; `[WB]` = the colleague's whiteboard matrix; `[MV]` = verified by the map owner or members. Light‑blue rows are employers added by research that were not on the owner's original contractor list; orange rows are assets not on the Key Assets tab.

- **onshore** — the owner's original mapping. One row per *company at an onshore facility*: Location, Facility, Company, Role (the operator row is marked "Tier 1"; contractor rows sit beneath it), plus organising‑tracking columns (scope, worker#, member#, EBA status, eba expiry, other unions?) that are mostly still blank. Facilities: Gorgon/Barrow Island (incl. CO2 and JIC construction), Wheatstone (Onslow), Karratha Gas Plant, Pluto 1 and 2, Varanus Island (Santos onshore and offshore), Ichthys Darwin, plus placeholders (Waitsia, DLNG).
- **Offshore - Key Assets** — 43 offshore production assets (platforms, FPSOs, FLNG, FPU, CPF, subsea developments), one row each: Tier 1 operator · **Facility / vessel operator** · Asset / Facility · Field(s) · Asset type · Sub‑sector · Status · the same tracking columns. The *Facility / vessel operator* column was added on 22 Sep 2026 to hold the day‑to‑day operator where it is a distinct designation from the titleholder — MODEC on Pyrenees Venture, BW Offshore on BW Opal, "(self‑op)" where the titleholder runs its own floating facility, "(historical)" for MODEC's past vessels, "—" for fixed platforms and subsea. Cell comments record the changes made (Pyrenees Tier‑1 set to Woodside; Barossa Tier‑1 set to Santos for consistency; Scarborough first‑gas date corrected to 29 Jul 2026; Ningaloo Vision note). Scope is national: WA, NT/Timor Sea (Barossa, Bayu‑Undan, Montara), Bass Strait (Woodside‑operated from 1 Jul 2026), Otway, plus planned assets (Browse, Sunrise, Dorado, Corvus) and decommissioning assets.
- **Offshore - key clients** — the eight major marine/subsea contractors the owner chose to track (Saipem, Allseas, Subsea7, McDermott, Fugro, DeepOcean/Shelf Subsea, Van Oord, Vantris ex‑Sapura): 38 vessels and shore facilities, each with the NW‑Australia project and Tier‑1 client it served, vessel type, service line, **presence** (Current / Recent 2023–25 / Likely‑upcoming / Historical / fabrication‑only), Australian base, notes, sources. Vessels are transient; presence is a snapshot.
- **Offshore - contractors** — the owner's original scope × company list: ten scopes (Marine, Chemist, Drilling, Catering, Surveyors, Maintenance, Helicopters, ROV, Construction, Hookup/Commissioning, Decomm) and 39 companies, several appearing under multiple scopes (Programmed under eight; OSM five; AOS four). Some entries are abbreviations or misspellings that have since been resolved (see the linkages tab).
- **Offshore - contractor linkages** — the researched *contractor footprint*: 55 rows (the 39 plus 16 added employers), one per company: identified legal/trading entity; the owner's scope label and a scope check; onshore facilities (pulled from the onshore tab); offshore key assets linked (canonical asset names, each with confidence); Tier‑1 clients; links to the eight marine key clients; overall confidence; evidence notes (contract awards, enterprise‑agreement titles, project pages); sources. Name resolutions matter for matching: MEA = Monadelphous Engineering Associates; "Hjelix" = Helix; "Enemech" = EnerMech; "Sierra" = Siera Marine Management; "Cyan" = Cyan Renewables (ex‑MMA Offshore); AOS = Australian Offshore Solutions; OSA = Offshore Services Australasia (ex‑Babcock); UPS = Upstream Production Solutions; "WPF (Duratech)" = WPF Duratec (Wilson's Pipe Fabrication); Eris = ex‑Kuiper Australia; Atlas Professionals = legacy name of Programmed Offshore; GGC = GGC Industries (low confidence).
- **Offshore - contractor x Tier-1** — the same 55 companies as an H/M/L matrix against ten operator columns (the eight above plus Esso/Bass Strait, now folded to Woodside, and the Australian Government for the Northern Endeavour decommissioning).
- **Offshore - asset -> contractors** — the inverse view: one row per asset (43 + three decommissioning assets not on the Key Assets tab: Northern Endeavour, Harriet Alpha, Campbell) listing every linked company by category (marine key client / contractor / added employer) with confidence and source tag. Eight assets have no standing employer linkage — all subsea, normally‑unmanned, planned or ceased.
- **Offshore - operator companies** — a long, relational table: one row per *operator–company pair* (~172 rows) with Tier (1 / 2 / Other), operator, company, category (marine key client / offshore contractor / added employer / onshore contractor), scope, **basis** (explicit contract/EA link, "via asset", or the onshore tab), the assets that link them, onshore facilities, confidence and a note. Where an asset has both a titleholder and a distinct facility operator (Barossa: Santos and BW Offshore; Pyrenees: Woodside and MODEC), companies attribute to both, with the note saying so.

Normally‑unmanned installations show no standing caterer by design.

## 3. Modelling distinctions the data forces

These are observations about what the mapping data *is*, offered as considerations for how the database represents it.

**Two operators per asset, not one.** A titleholder/operator (the Tier‑1) and a day‑to‑day facility or vessel operator are different roles that coincide for most fixed platforms but diverge for floating facilities: Woodside is titleholder of Pyrenees while MODEC operates and self‑performs O&M (including catering positions); Santos operates the Barossa field while BW Offshore owns and operates the BW Opal FPSO under a 15‑year charter. The owner's Tier 1 / Tier 2 classification treats MODEC and BW Opal as operators in their own right. Contractor relationships attach to one or the other (Monadelphous's Barossa maintenance contract is with BW Offshore; the Barossa pipelay and drilling contracts were with Santos), so operator attribution is a *role on a relationship*, not a property of the asset alone. Historical vessel operators (MODEC on Stybarrow or Mutineer‑Exeter) should not inherit today's contractor relationships.

**Contractor engagements are role‑specific to the facility.** The same company plays different roles at different facilities and under different principals. The natural grain is an *engagement*: organisation × facility (or vessel) × scope × role (incumbent, subcontractor, crew provider) × principal (which operator or which prime contractor) × validity window × confidence × source. A company‑level "scope" attribute is a summary of these, not a substitute.

**The employer of record is often one layer behind the incumbent.** Crewing and labour‑hire firms — AOS, OSM, Siera, Programmed/Atlas, Eris, GR Production Services — supply the people who work on vessels and facilities nominally held by others: AOS crews and caters Saipem's Castorone and McDermott's Scarborough FPU tow; Siera crews Tidewater's PSVs, DOF's Skandi Hercules and McDermott's DLV 2000; OSM crews Sea1 vessels and the former Tidewater ships; Eris supplies construction and maintenance crews under Technip, Allseas, Saipem and Subsea7 contracts. A Tidewater vessel can carry three distinct employer relationships. For organising, and for enterprise‑agreement coverage, the distinction between *incumbent contractor*, *principal*, and *employing entity* is load‑bearing, and prime contractors such as Technip Energies (the Crux HUC principal above Monadelphous and Eris) can be the relevant node even when they are not on any contractor list.

**Corporate identity churns; agreement‑holding entities are granular.** The map records rebrands and acquisitions within the last three years (Kuiper → Eris; MMA Offshore → Cyan Renewables; Sapura → Vantris; Siem Offshore Australia → Sea1; Atlas Professionals → Programmed Offshore; Babcock offshore helicopters → OSA within CHC; Tidewater Ship Management → OSM/TSM Offshore; Diamond Offshore → Noble; a pending Transocean–Valaris merger), and groups whose enterprise agreements sit with specific subsidiaries (Monadelphous: MWOG, M&ISS, M Maintenance Services, Monadelphous Engineering Associates; Compass: ESS; Programmed: Programmed Offshore, Programmed Marine, RFM Offshore). Matching employers in the database therefore depends on a canonical organisation with aliases, parent/subsidiary structure, and an explicit link from each enterprise agreement to the entity that holds it, rather than on name equality.

**Everything is time‑bound.** Asset statuses move (Scarborough went from construction to first gas on 29 Jul 2026; Ningaloo Vision and Reindeer ceased in 2025; Bayu‑Undan ceased pending CCS conversion), operatorship transfers (Bass Strait from Esso to Woodside on 1 Jul 2026; Julimar‑Brunello from Woodside to Chevron around Q4 2026), contracts have windows and options, helicopter and catering incumbents change hands (Prelude helicopters CHC → PHI in Sept 2025), and vessel presence is a snapshot (Current / Recent / Likely). Overwriting a status or operator loses the history the map is trying to capture; effective‑dated relationships preserve it and make "who was on this facility in 2024" answerable.

**Confidence and provenance are attributes of each fact.** Every link in the derived tabs carries H/M/L and a source tag, and the reconciliation tab shows why: two "facts" from research were corrected by a whiteboard and a member (Scarborough's date; Prelude's caterer), other whiteboard entries were adopted at Medium, and some were removed after verification. The map is explicitly a *reconciled working hypothesis*, not a ledger, and the most authoritative source for facility coverage — the coverage clauses of Fair Work Commission enterprise agreements, which name platforms, FPSOs, rigs and vessels — has not yet been read into it. A record of source, date and verification status per relationship is what lets research, organiser knowledge and member verification converge without one silently overwriting another.

**Assets, fields, hubs and worksites are not the same grain.** The map holds facilities as rows, but fields feed facilities (Vincent and Greater Enfield both produce through Ngujima‑Yin; Julimar‑Brunello produces through Chevron's Wheatstone platform), hubs group facilities (the Varanus Island hub: John Brookes, Spar/Greater East Spar, Halyard, Spartan, with Varanus Island itself onshore), offshore assets pair with onshore plants (NWS platforms → Karratha Gas Plant; Ichthys CPF → Bladin Point; Barossa → Darwin LNG), and normally‑unmanned installations (Angel, Pluto A, John Brookes, Crux) have no standing workforce at all — their organising relevance is campaign‑based. A *worksite* for organising purposes may be a facility, a hub, an onshore plant, a campaign, or a vessel; the database's worksite concept needs to accommodate more than one of these grains, and the onshore/offshore pairing is itself a relationship worth keeping.

**Vessels are mobile worksites.** The marine key‑client tab treats vessels as first‑class assets with an owner/operator, a type, a stable identity (IMO number — e.g. Fugro Etive, IMO 9379686), a base (Perth‑based fleets such as Fugro and DeepOcean/Shelf Subsea versus transient mobilisations by Saipem, Allseas, Van Oord and Vantris), and time‑bound presence in the NW region tied to a project and principal. Their crews' employers (see above) and their enterprise agreements attach to the vessel or the vessel operator rather than to any fixed facility.

**Geography and jurisdiction vary by asset.** Assets sit in Commonwealth waters off WA, off the NT, in the Timor Sea under Australian permits (Montara), in Timor‑Leste's jurisdiction (Bayu‑Undan, and MODEC's historical Buffalo and Elang vessels), and in Bass Strait and the Otway Basin off Victoria. The colleague's matrix was deliberately WA‑only; the map is national. Region is therefore a property that filters and scopes without being an identity.

## 4. How the map relates to the database's existing concepts

The database already models *worksites*, *agreement–worksite junctions* and *employer role types*, and reconciles member records against Fair Work Commission agreement identifiers. Seen against that structure:

- The map's **assets, vessels and onshore facilities are candidate worksites**, at more than one grain (facility, hub, campaign, vessel), with the onshore–offshore pairing and the field→facility feed as relationships between worksites.
- The map's **Tier‑1 / facility‑operator / contractor / crew‑provider / prime‑contractor / marine‑contractor distinctions are candidate employer role types**, and a single organisation can hold different roles at different worksites and dates.
- The **facility × scope incumbents, and the enterprise‑agreement titles cited in the linkages tab, are the natural feed for agreement–worksite junctions**: an agreement held by a specific entity, covering named facilities or vessels, for a scope, over a period. The coverage clauses of those agreements are the authoritative source for which worksites each junction should include.
- The map's **confidence and source tags** have no counterpart in a pure ledger and are the mechanism by which organiser and member knowledge can be layered onto researched structure — the same mechanism the member reconciliation already needs when a member's employer name does not match an agreement's legal entity.

## 5. Known limitations of the mapping data

It is research‑derived and a snapshot as at September 2026. Several abbreviations from the original contractor list were resolved by inference (GGC remains uncertain; "EEIS" on the colleague's whiteboard is presumed to be Eris). Enterprise‑agreement coverage clauses were not read. Eight assets have no employer linkage. The Bass Strait platforms are represented as a single summary row pending a dedicated sub‑map. The catering and maintenance incumbents at Stag and Montara, the long‑term maintenance contractor for Scarborough, the Barrow Island camp caterer and Entier's facility are unresolved in both this map and the colleague's. Vessel presence and helicopter/catering incumbencies change quickly and should be treated as dated observations.
